My T. Thai

My T. Thai is the Research Foundation Professor of Computer & Information Sciences & Engineering, the Nelms Endowed Professor, and Associate Director of the Nelms Institute for the Connected World at the University of Florida. Her research develops the mathematical and algorithmic foundations for understanding, optimizing, and securing intelligent systems, from large-scale networks to modern AI. Her current work spans the interpretability and reliability of foundation models, privacy-preserving distributed learning, efficient and secure multi-agent AI, and quantum learning and optimization. Her work has applications in healthcare, cybersecurity, blockchains, and critical networking infrastructures.

Dr. Thai is a Fellow of the IEEE. Her honors include the NSF CAREER Award, the DTRA Young Investigator Award, the AAAI 2023 Distinguished Paper Award, and the Web Science Trust Test of Time Award in 2023. Her work also received a USENIX Security 2026 Distinguished Paper Runner-up Award.

Dr. Thai serves as Editor-in-Chief of ACM Computing Surveys and the Journal of Combinatorial Optimization, and Founding Editor-in-Chief of IET Blockchain. She is also a book series editor for Springer Optimization and Its Applications and SpringerBriefs in Optimization.

Research Overview

I lead Thái Lab (The Science of Intelligent Systems), where we explore how models learn, agents interact, and failures arise. We ask how to uncover the mechanisms behind learned behavior, coordinate decisions efficiently under limited resources, and establish reliability, privacy, and security guarantees under uncertainty and adversarial conditions.

We address these questions through mathematical analysis, algorithm design, and experimental validation. For more information, visit the Thái Lab website.
